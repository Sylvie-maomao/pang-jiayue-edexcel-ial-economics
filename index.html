Tax essentials · 考试核心知识

Specific tax vs ad valorem tax

税楔的计征方式决定供给曲线的变化形态。

Specific / unit tax · 从量税

Ps = a + bQ
Pc = (a + T) + bQ
Slope remains b
  • Definition: fixed money amount per unit
  • Fixed wedge: Pc(Q) − Ps(Q) = T
  • Intercept changes; slope remains b; vertical gap stays T

每个 Q 都在原供给价格上加同一个固定金额 T，因此纵向距离不变。

Ad valorem tax · 从价税

Ps = a + bQ
Pc = (1 + τ)(a + bQ)
Slope becomes (1 + τ)b
  • Definition: percentage of the product's value
  • Variable wedge: Pc(Q) − Ps(Q) = τPs(Q)
  • Intercept and slope multiply by 1 + τ → pivot/divergence

每个 Q 的税额都等于原供给价格乘以税率；价格越高，纵向距离越大。

公式说明：从量税采用加法形式 P + T，增加量恒定；从价税采用乘法形式 (1 + τ)P，增加量随 P 扩大。
Pearson Student Book 1, Chapter 13, pp.90–91; Edexcel IAL Economics Specification 1.3.4.
One supply curve · Same Q · Different tax rule

基于同一 P-Q 供给函数比较两类税收

统一设原供给曲线为 Ps(Q) = Q。含义：供给 Q 单位时，生产者每单位至少要保留价格 Q。

图形说明：横轴表示数量 Q，纵轴表示价格 P。选定任意数量 Q，原曲线 S 给出 Ps(Q)；税后曲线给出消费者必须支付的 Pc(Q)。两条曲线在同一 Q 上的纵向距离等于单位税额。

从量税：T = £5

Ps(Q) = Q
Pc(Q) = Ps(Q) + 5 = Q + 5
在 Q = 10：Ps = £10，Pc = £15，税额 = £5。
在 Q = 20：Ps = £20，Pc = £25，税额仍为 £5。

结论：Pc(Q) − Ps(Q) = 5，与 Q 无关；两条线斜率都为 1，各个 Q 上的纵向距离恒定。

从价税：τ = 25%

Ps(Q) = Q
Pc(Q) = 1.25Ps(Q) = 1.25Q
在 Q = 10：Ps = £10，税额 = 25% × £10 = £2.5，Pc = £12.5。
在 Q = 20：Ps = £20，税额 = 25% × £20 = £5，Pc = £25。

结论：税楔 = 0.25Q，随 Q 对应的 Ps 上升；原线斜率为 1，税后斜率为 1.25，所以曲线向上旋转并发散。

Q = 10£10£5£15£2.5£12.5

判别规则：固定金额 → 各个 Q 上增加相同金额；百分比税率 → 税楔扩大，曲线旋转或发散。题目表述为“£x per unit / per litre / per packet”时，单位税额不随 P、Q 改变；题目表述为“x% / VAT / ad valorem”时，单位税额 = τP，P 越高税额越大。

Weekly consolidation · Diagram and logic

Read every tax diagram through four outcomes

分析税收图形时，应依次考察价格、数量、税负与政府收入。

Incidence and revenue

  • Consumer incidence per unit = Pc − P₀
  • Producer incidence per unit = P₀ − Pp
  • Tax per unit = Pc − Pp
  • Government revenue = (Pc − Pp) × Q₁

税负由价格变化决定；政府收入是“税楔 × 征税后交易量”，不是原交易量。

Elasticity decides burden

The less price-elastic side of the market bears more of the tax. Inelastic demand therefore gives consumers a larger incidence; inelastic supply gives producers a larger incidence.

弹性较小的一方承担更多税负。需求缺乏弹性时消费者承担更多；供给缺乏弹性时生产者承担更多。

Surplus and welfare

Consumer surplus and producer surplus fall. Tax revenue is a transfer, not welfare loss. Only the remaining net loss from trades that no longer occur is welfare loss.

消费者剩余和生产者剩余下降，税收收入属于转移，并非福利损失；因交易消失而产生的净损失才是福利损失。

Evaluation test

A tax can reduce harmful consumption and raise revenue, but its effectiveness depends on elasticity, substitution, information, enforcement costs and the use of revenue.

税收可能减少有害消费并增加收入，但效果取决于弹性、替代行为、信息、执行成本以及收入用途。

Pearson Edexcel IAL Economics Specification 1.3.4(4) and 1.3.6.
Production externalities · MPC–MSC diagnosis

MPC 与 MSC 谁在上面？三步判断正负生产外部性

曲线本身不叫“积极”或“消极”；同一数量 Q 上两条成本曲线的高低，决定生产外部性的方向。

先固定同一个 Q，再计算成本差

Δ(Q) = MSC(Q) − MPC(Q)

Δ > 0 → 社会成本高于私人成本 → 负生产外部性；Δ = 0 → 无生产外部性；Δ < 0 → 社会成本低于私人成本 → 正生产外部性。

负生产外部性 · MSC 在上

判断：MSC(Q) − MPC(Q) = MEC(Q) > 0。
含义：企业没有承担污染、噪音等第三方成本。
结果：Qm > Q*，市场过度生产。

正生产外部性 · MSC 在下

判断：MPC(Q) − MSC(Q) > 0，生产产生外部收益。
含义：社会获得企业未计入决策的技术扩散、培训等收益。
结果：Qm < Q*，市场生产不足。
同一个 Q 上的关系成本差生产外部性市场结果
MSC > MPCMSC − MPC = MEC > 0Negative production externalityQm > Q*，过度生产
MSC = MPC成本差 = 0No production externalityQm = Q*
MSC < MPCMPC − MSC = 外部收益 > 0Positive production externalityQm < Q*，生产不足
三步口诀：固定 Q → 看 MSC 在 MPC 上方还是下方 → 上方是外部成本、下方是外部收益。不要根据曲线斜率判断，也不要把 MPC 或 MSC 本身称为“正”或“负”。

20-mark method · Official-descriptor synthesis

A repeatable structure for “Evaluate”

Pearson does not prescribe a paragraph formula; this structure operationalises its public level descriptors.

Opening and diagram

  1. Define the exact policy or concept.
  2. Classify it precisely: specific or ad valorem.
  3. Use one stem fact immediately.
  4. Draw, shift, label and explain the diagram.

KAA chain × 2 or 3

Change → market mechanism → price/quantity or incentive → stakeholder effect → wider microeconomic consequence.

Integrate a number, named market or institution inside each chain. A quoted number without explanation is not developed application.

Evaluation chain × 2 or 3

Condition → why it changes magnitude or direction → contextual evidence or evidence limit → revised conclusion.

Evaluation must explain why a condition changes the magnitude or direction of the result. Useful tests include elasticity, time, distribution, substitution and enforcement.

Final judgement

Answer the question directly. State the most likely effect, the decisive condition and the missing evidence. Do not merely repeat both sides.

Compact paragraph pattern: claim → because → therefore → context → however → because → mini-judgement.
Derived from Pearson's public WEC11 October 2021 mark scheme and official Unit 1 exemplars.

Model answer · Uganda mobile internet tax

The 200-shilling tax raises supply by a constant vertical amount

The vertical distance between S and S + tax is 200 shillings per user-day.

Diagram chain

The fixed daily tax raises marginal cost by the same amount at every quantity. Supply shifts from S to S + tax without pivoting. Consumer price rises from P₀ to Pc, producers receive Pp net of tax, and usage contracts from Q₀ to Q₁.

Context to integrate

  • 200 shillings per day
  • Participation: 47.4% → 35%
  • 12.4 percentage-point fall
  • Facebook, Twitter and Instagram
Opening thesis: participation clearly fell, but the effect on net government revenue and total welfare is uncertain.
Pearson official October 2021 Q13 mark scheme: “specific tax ... supply shifts and does not pivot”.
Model answer · Uganda · KAA

Precise definition, integrated data, multi-stage effects

An indirect tax is a tax on expenditure imposed when a good or service is purchased. Uganda's charge was a specific tax because each consumer paid a fixed 200 shillings per day, rather than a percentage of the value of internet use. It therefore raised the consumer-facing supply price by the same 200 shillings at every quantity.

In the diagram, the tax raises the consumer price from P₀ to Pc, lowers the producer's net price to Pp and contracts usage from Q₀ to Q₁. This is consistent with participation falling from 47.4% to 35%, a decline of 12.4 percentage points. Consumer surplus falls because users pay more and some leave the market. Producer surplus and possibly profits also fall because the quantity of taxed access declines and providers retain only Pp after tax.

Lower demand for services including Facebook, Twitter and Instagram may reduce advertising reach and revenue. Mobile-network operators may respond by reducing investment or employment if expected returns in Uganda fall. Therefore the tax can affect linked digital markets as well as the direct market for mobile internet access.

Government revenue = (Pc − Pp) × Q₁: the 200-shilling tax multiplied by taxed user-days. If collection costs are low, this could finance infrastructure or support Ugandan firms. Better digital or transport infrastructure may reduce firms' costs and create external benefits, so some of the loss of private surplus could be converted into wider social benefits through government spending.

However, mobile internet may itself create positive externalities. Access to information, online education, communication and digital business can raise the productivity of people beyond the individual buyer. By making access more expensive, the tax may reduce these benefits. Fewer users may also make online advertising less valuable to local firms, reducing sales and employment. These effects make the tax less likely to increase total welfare even if it raises revenue.

The fixed daily charge may also be regressive. A 200-shilling payment is likely to take a larger proportion of a low-income user's budget than of a high-income user's budget. Poorer households may therefore be more likely to disconnect, widening unequal access to information and communication. This distributional effect is a microeconomic cost not shown by the simple market diagram.

Model answer · Uganda · Evaluation and judgement

The observed fall proves a response, not policy success

The magnitude depends first on price elasticity of demand. If demand is price inelastic because internet access has few close substitutes, consumer usage falls proportionately less than price rises. Consumers then bear more of the tax and government revenue is likely to be high. If demand is elastic, perhaps because users can use Wi-Fi, share accounts or stop using social media, Q₁ falls sharply, providers bear more of the burden and the revenue base contracts.

The participation data suggest a substantial behavioural response: the share using mobile internet fell by 12.4 percentage points, or about 26% relative to 47.4%. Nevertheless, this does not measure price elasticity because the question gives neither the percentage price increase nor other factors affecting demand. It also reports the share of people using the service, not the minutes used or the number of taxable days. The data therefore cannot establish the amount of revenue collected.

Unintended consequences may create government failure. Users may evade the charge through illegal access, while collection and monitoring create administrative costs. If these costs exceed the benefits of intervention, the policy produces net welfare loss. The tax may also discourage digital investment or block valuable information, outcomes inconsistent with long-run productivity growth.

On the other hand, Uganda may still collect considerable revenue from the 35% of the population that remains connected. Global platforms may experience only a small fall in total profit because Uganda is a small part of their worldwide market. The domestic welfare effect could therefore be much larger than the effect on Facebook, Twitter or Instagram as global firms.

The outcome also depends on how revenue is used. Spending it on reliable internet infrastructure, education or support for domestic firms may offset some lost surplus. If it is misdirected, not ring-fenced or absorbed by administration, the gross revenue rectangle overstates the policy's benefit. Distribution matters too: a fixed daily tax is more likely to exclude low-income users even when higher-income users continue consuming.

Judgement. The most certain effect is reduced participation and lower consumer surplus: the fall from 47.4% to 35% supports this. The policy may have raised gross revenue, but its success at raising net revenue cannot be confirmed without data on taxable user-days, evasion and administrative cost. Overall welfare is likely to fall if demand is elastic among poorer users and mobile access generates significant external benefits. The tax is more defensible if demand is inelastic, collection is cheap and the revenue is productively invested, but those conditions are not established by the evidence.

Model answer · Romania · KAA

From tax definition to health, surplus and revenue effects

An indirect tax is a tax on expenditure imposed when a good is sold. Romania's tax is ad valorem because it is charged as a percentage of the value of sugary foods. Raising the rate from 9% to 19% is a 10-percentage-point increase. Since the money tax per unit becomes larger at higher prices, the tax-inclusive supply curve rotates upwards from S + 9% to S + 19%, with the vertical tax gap increasing as price rises.

In the diagram, the higher rate raises firms' marginal cost. Consumer price rises from P₉ to Pc, producer net price falls to Pp and quantity contracts from Q₉ to Q₁₉. Consumer surplus falls because remaining buyers pay more and some purchases no longer occur. Producer surplus and profits may also fall because firms sell fewer sugary foods and receive a lower net price.

The contraction may improve allocative efficiency if sugary-food consumption creates external costs. Diet-related obesity can impose healthcare costs on taxpayers and reduce workplace productivity. Obesity rose from 7.7% to 10.9% between 2000 and 2024, suggesting the potential external cost is significant. By making consumers face more of the social cost through a higher price, the tax may reduce overconsumption and move output towards the social optimum.

Consumers may substitute towards foods with less sugar, while firms gain an incentive to reformulate. This can improve diet and may reduce future obesity-related illness, lowering pressure on healthcare services. The policy may therefore change both consumer demand and producer behaviour over time.

Government revenue = the 19% tax wedge × Q₁₉. This revenue could finance healthcare, nutrition information or subsidies for healthier foods, reinforcing the reduction in external costs. However, gross revenue is not itself a net welfare gain: part of it is transferred from lost consumer and producer surplus, and collection has administrative costs.

The tax may reduce output, revenue and employment in Romanian sugary-food producers and retailers. These firms may pass much of the tax to consumers, accept lower margins, or change their product mix. The less price-elastic side of the market bears more of the tax burden. Low-income households could be disproportionately affected because food spending forms a larger share of their income.

Model answer · Romania · Evaluation and judgement

Effectiveness depends on response, substitution and evidence

Price elasticity of demand is decisive. If sugary foods are habitual purchases with few close substitutes, demand may be price inelastic. The price rise then causes only a small reduction in Q, so consumers bear more of the tax. Revenue may rise strongly, but the health effect is limited. If healthier foods are close and affordable substitutes, demand is more elastic, consumption falls further and the policy is more effective at reducing the external cost, although revenue may be lower than expected.

The tax is more regressive when demand is inelastic. Low-income households that continue buying sugary foods lose a larger share of real income. Subsidising fruit or reformulating products could reduce this trade-off. Some consumers may instead buy untaxed sugary substitutes, shop across borders or switch to products with other unhealthy ingredients. Such substitution would weaken the health gain without removing the burden.

The obesity evidence does not prove causation. The rise from 7.7% to 10.9% occurred over 24 years and may also reflect exercise, income, portion size, genetics and consumption of non-taxed foods. Obesity changes with a time lag, so the short-run fall in sugary-food purchases may not quickly reduce the measured obesity rate. The government may therefore set 19% above or below the tax that accurately reflects marginal external cost.

Government revenue is ambiguous rather than automatically higher. The rate more than doubles relative to 9%, but the tax base Q contracts. Revenue rises if the higher tax per unit outweighs the reduction in taxed sales. The net benefit is smaller if monitoring, classification and collection are costly or if revenue is not used for health-related purposes.

In the longer run, producers can reformulate and supply and demand may become more responsive. That can increase the health benefit while limiting job losses. Conversely, lobbying, avoidance or inaccurate nutritional information may create government failure. A tax alone cannot correct information gaps if consumers still underestimate health risks.

Judgement. The most likely immediate effects are a higher consumer price, lower producer net price and lower quantity. The policy is most likely to improve welfare when demand is sufficiently elastic, sugary foods are a major cause of Romania's external health costs, and revenue supports complementary health measures. If demand is inelastic, the principal result will instead be higher revenue and a regressive burden with only a small consumption change. The direction of the market effect is clear, but the size of the welfare gain is uncertain.
